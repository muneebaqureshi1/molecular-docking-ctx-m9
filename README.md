# Molecular Docking and Antibacterial Drug Discovery Against CTX-M-9 β-Lactamase

## Overview

This repository documents my master's research on structure-based drug discovery against CTX-M-9 β-lactamase, an antimicrobial resistance-associated enzyme.

The study combined molecular docking and computational analysis with experimental evaluation of selected compounds.

## Research Question

Can computational molecular docking help identify heterocyclic compounds with potential antibacterial activity against antimicrobial resistance-associated targets?

## Computational Workflow

The study involved:

1. Target protein selection and preparation
2. Ligand preparation
3. Molecular docking
4. Binding interaction analysis
5. Virtual screening
6. ADME prediction
7. Comparison of computational predictions with experimental antibacterial activity

## Target

**CTX-M-9 β-lactamase**

CTX-M enzymes are important mechanisms of β-lactam antibiotic resistance in Gram-negative bacteria.

## Compounds

A set of 50 heterocyclic compounds was investigated using molecular docking.

Selected compounds were subsequently evaluated experimentally to examine their antibacterial activity.

## Computational–Experimental Comparison

An important aspect of the research was comparing molecular docking predictions with experimental results.

Some compounds showed favourable computational predictions and antibacterial activity, while other results demonstrated that docking scores alone could not fully predict experimental activity.

This highlighted the importance of combining computational approaches with experimental validation.

## Experimental Validation

Selected compounds were evaluated for antibacterial activity against bacterial strains including:

* *Escherichia coli*

The experimental results were compared with the computational predictions.
