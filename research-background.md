# Research Background

## Antimicrobial Resistance

Antimicrobial resistance is a major challenge in infectious disease treatment. β-lactamase enzymes are an important mechanism of resistance because they can hydrolyze β-lactam antibiotics.

## CTX-M-9 β-Lactamase

My master's research focused on CTX-M-9 β-lactamase as a potential target for antibacterial drug discovery.

The aim was to investigate whether selected heterocyclic compounds could interact favourably with the enzyme and show antibacterial activity.

## Computational Approach

Molecular docking was used to investigate the possible binding of compounds to the target protein.

The computational workflow included protein and ligand preparation, docking, analysis of protein–ligand interactions, virtual screening, and ADME prediction.

## Experimental Approach

Computational predictions were followed by experimental antibacterial evaluation.

This allowed comparison between predicted molecular interactions and observed antibacterial activity.

## Key Research Insight

The research demonstrated that favourable docking results do not necessarily guarantee experimental antibacterial activity.

Conversely, experimentally active compounds may not always be identified as the strongest candidates by docking alone.

This computational–experimental gap increased my interest in developing approaches that combine computational prediction with experimental feedback.

## Future Research Direction

I am interested in developing computational skills in Python, machine learning, and data-driven drug discovery and applying them alongside experimental approaches to antimicrobial resistance.
